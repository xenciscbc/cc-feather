# Resource risk is marked at plan review and contained by the Runner

Until 0.31.0 nothing in delegation judged whether running a claim's checks could exhaust memory, time or the machine: a role ran the tests and probes its Execution allowed, nothing in its definition or brief spoke of resources, and nothing on the machine limited what a run could take. In another project, a probe a verifier invented during verification compared against a reference implementation without a step limit and grew without bound, although its outer loop was bounded; the risk had been visible in that project's spec. A design of 2026-11-07 judged the risk only at each run.

The user decided (2026-11-08, after a design discussion and Codex second opinions; the [spec](../specs/resource-risk-runs.md) carries the acceptance) on four layers, main's approval and the Runner forming the second together, with budgets that come from the user, the Plan or the user's guidance and never from cc-feather:

- **Plan review marks the risk.** Plan review judges each Claim's resource risk as one item of its checklist. For a Claim whose checks or verification foreseeably risk exhausting memory, time or the machine, the analyst marks it a Resource-risk claim, estimates its peak with the basis, and proposes an Execution method for the risky part. A missing mark is not a statement that a Claim is safe.
- **Main approves the method with a budget.** Before the first run of a Resource-risk claim, main approves the Execution method, changes it or asks the user, with a budget taken in order from the user's own statement in the session, the Plan's execution conditions, the project's resource guidance and the user's resource guidance, and asks the user when none states one or the need exceeds a default. The approval is bound to the Claim, its reading set, the experiment and the budget, and is carried into every brief of a role that may execute code for the Claim and into main's own runs. It adds to Execution and never replaces it.
- **The Runner contains the risky part.** A role runs the risky part only as approved and only inside the Runner, cc-feather's launcher that puts a memory limit on the whole process tree, a time limit and an output limit, and refuses to start rather than run without them. The role never raises a limit, still judges whether the Claim holds and whether the method can establish it, and otherwise reports INCONCLUSIVE with its own proposed method for main to decide in a new, fresh, normally counted call.
- **A floor for every run.** A new or modified test or probe, whoever wrote it, whose bound is unknown or does not fit the budget is an Unbounded probe: it is not run and goes back to main. The floor follows execution, not authorship, so it covers unmarked Claims, `off` and main's own reruns and reproductions.
- **What happened is recorded.** A returned probe or a run a limit stopped becomes an Observed resource risk on the Claim, kept outside the Claim's text; main tells the user, carries it into later briefs of that Claim and lists it at the end of the Plan.

Main's side is the [resource-risk reference](../../skills/delegation/references/resource-risk-runs.md), the plan-review item is in the analyst definition, the role's rule is in the verifier, adversary, executor and security-executor definitions, and the terms are in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md).

## Considered Options

- **Judging risk only at each run**: the design of 2026-11-07. The role that misjudged a probe stays the only judge of it, and a risk visible in the Plan is not caught before anything runs.
- **A two-phase paused call**: the role proposes its method in the middle of a call and continues after main's approval. It needs a new paused state in review counting; an approved method before one fresh call, with disagreement returned as INCONCLUSIVE, keeps counting as it is.
- **A separate resource-analysis command**: a command or skill of its own for the judgement. Plan review already reads the Plan before anything runs, so the judgement is one item there and adds no verdict of its own, and an explicit plan review gives `off` a way in without a new command.
- **Enforcing Runner use with a hook now**: a probe on Claude Code 2.1.319 showed it feasible, but binding a call to its Claim, failing closed, preserving permissions and shell equivalence need their own validation; it is left to a later, separately gated release.
- **A watchdog**: a process that watches the session's processes and stops runaway ones. In the field notes the spec relies on, a watchdog stopped nothing, while the one limit hit was contained by a runner.

## Consequences

Only processes inside the Runner's containment are limited: work handed to an already running service and tools that execute code outside the shell are outside it, and two runs started at the same time each check available memory on their own, so their total is not limited. The Runner supports Windows and Linux and refuses elsewhere, and on an environment where it refuses, a Resource-risk claim stops for the user to decide. A Runner invocation goes through the host's normal permission handling, and cc-feather advises against a rule that allows every Runner invocation. The rules are instructions to the model: no hook enforces that a role uses the Runner, and the tests and the one live rehearsal recorded under 0.32.0 in [setup validation](../setup-validation.md#0320-resource-risk-runs) cannot show that a model always follows them. 0.32.0 changes role definitions, so existing installations need setup update.
